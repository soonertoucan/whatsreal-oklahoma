# Research behind What's Real Oklahoma

Compiled October 25, 2026. Nine days before the general election. This is the deep dive
the site was built from. Each section ends with the sources used and what could not be
verified. Where a figure comes from advocacy or a single outlet, it is marked.

## 1. Where things stand, in one paragraph

Oklahoma votes November 3, 2026. Early voting is Wednesday October 28 through Saturday
October 31. Registration closed October 9 and absentee requests closed October 19, so the
site's job is turnout and persuasion among people already registered, not registration.
Nationally the president's approval is at 31% (Reuters/Ipsos, Oct 19), the generic
congressional ballot is roughly D+7 to D+12 depending on the pollster, and the issue
driving it is cost of living: inflation at 3.7% and gas at $4.28, both pushed by the
Iran war's effect on oil. In Oklahoma the open governor's race between Mike Mazzei (R) and
Cyndi Munson (D) is inside the margin of error in the only public poll (Cross Timbers,
Oct 7–12, n=128, so treat it lightly). The U.S. Senate special election (Hern vs. Thomas)
and all five House seats are rated safe Republican except the 5th District (Bice vs.
Nelson, "Likely R," one campaign poll at 48–43). Oklahoma had the lowest turnout in the
nation in 2024 (53% of eligible adults). Turnout, not conversion, is the larger lever.

## 2. What the data says is hurting Oklahomans

### Health care
- 63 of 73 rural hospitals at risk of closing, 18 immediately (CHQPR analysis via KFOR).
- Oklahoma Hospital Association: at least $6.7B lost federal Medicaid funding under H.R. 1,
  plus ~$1B to physicians; 15,000–18,000 health jobs; up to 300,000 fewer insured. The jobs
  and insured figures are OHA projections quoted secondhand; treat as estimates.
- Integris Health cutting doctors and clinics on a projected $130M/yr loss (OKC Fox 25).
- ~14,000 children already off Medicaid after 2025 changes (Tulsa Flyer).
- SoonerCare: ~1.0M enrolled, 237,525 expansion adults, ~531,000 children (OHCA Fast
  Facts, April 2026). Primary source.
- ACA Marketplace: enhanced credits expired end of 2025. 2026 premiums +20–21% average;
  ~50,000 fewer plan selections; ~1 in 7 enrollees dropped (Oklahoma Insurance Department
  via KOSU). 2027 filings +15% to +22% (rate filings; OID public estimate lower). Open
  enrollment shortened to Nov 1–Dec 15.

### Cost of living
- CPI 3.7% y/y September 2026, up from 3.4%; gasoline +9.1% in the month, over half the
  increase; groceries −0.1% (BLS via CNN, Oct 14). Next CPI Nov 10.
- Gas $4.28 national average week of Oct 19 vs $2.78 in January; diesel record $6.53 the
  week of Sept 21 (EIA via FactCheck.org).
- Airfares +22.4% y/y (BLS).
- A Joint Economic Committee minority estimate of $4,700+ extra per household since 2025
  exists but is partisan; not used on the site.
- State grocery tax (4.5%) eliminated Aug 29, 2024. Savings estimates range from ~$125/yr
  for a $40k family (OK Policy) to ~$800 (legislators). Both are shown.

### Farms and rural economy
- China soybean purchases −78%; Oklahoma farm fuel costs +50%+; fertilizer up on tariffs
  and the Hormuz closure (Oklahoma Watch, May 11, 2026). Cattle at record prices.
- Wheat ~$6 received vs ~$10.50 cost for one farmer (KGOU, Jul 16); USDA forecast $6.30.
- ~18% of Oklahoma-based federal ag workforce gone in H1 2025 (USDA OIG via KGOU).
- SNAP cost shift: ~$240M/yr in benefits from Oct 2027 (OK error rate 11.04%, top tier);
  75% of admin costs from Oct 1, 2026 ($25M added to state budget). DHS director: nearly
  $500M total (Hunger Free Oklahoma; Oklahoma Voice; KOSU).

### Taxes and state budget
- Federal transfers were 32.2% of Oklahoma state and local revenue in FY2023, ~$17B, seven
  points above the national average (Census via USAFacts). Oklahoma is more exposed to
  federal cuts than most states.
- HB 2764 (2025) cut the top income tax rate 4.75% → 4.5% with revenue triggers for further
  cuts. FY2026 collections +$142M vs prior year (state figures, cited by OCPA; OCPA is a
  conservative think tank, the underlying numbers are the state's).
- SQ 847 (property valuation caps): UCO estimate $42M year one to $575M/yr by year ten in
  lost local revenue (NonDoc). Farm Bureau supports. Does not cap bills, only valuations.
- SQ 845 (Judicial Nominating Commission): removes party-balance and attorney limits,
  12-year terms. Robert Henry opposes; proponents cite more qualified attorneys.

### Schools
- NEA 2026: Oklahoma starting teacher pay $41,294 (48th); average $61,931 (37th).
- SB 201 added $2,000 to the minimum salary schedule; no guaranteed raise above schedule.

## 4. Who is on the ballot and what they say

**Governor.** Mazzei (R): eliminate income tax in six years (4.5% → 3% now, consumer
activity tax by referendum for the rest); abolish property tax for seniors and veterans;
literacy director and $150M phonics; keep Parental Choice Tax Credit; CareerTech
expansion; "address rural health care and insurance costs" with no published plan; no
stated position found on Medicaid expansion. Munson (D): opposes across-the-board income
tax cuts, targeted Sales Tax Relief Credit and EITC; repeal Parental Choice credit; more
school funding and pay; Oklahoma's Promise expansion; free school meals; keep Medicaid
expansion in the constitution; route Rural Health Transformation money to small hospitals;
day-one pause on new data centers; repeal abortion ban. Independents Brooks, Bush,
Griffin: no verified positions found.

**U.S. Senate (special).** Hern (R): voted YES on H.R. 1 (Roll Call 190, Jul 3, 2025);
~$10M raised. Thomas (D): military spouse; democratic socialist, not DSA; Medicare for
All, universal childcare, paid leave; does not support cutting defense; ~$60K raised.
Libertarian Sevier White; independents Meinhardt, Stinnett declared.

**U.S. House.** OK-1 Tedford (R) vs Croisant (D); OK-2 Brecheen (R, YES on H.R. 1) vs Wade
(D); OK-3 Lucas (R, YES) vs Byrd (D); OK-4 Cole (R, YES) vs Jacob (D); OK-5 Bice (R, YES) vs
Nelson (D: cost of living first, protect Medicare/Medicaid, insulin cap, restore Dept. of

Oklahoma Republicans and both senators voted for H.R. 1. Wade, Byrd and Jacob had no

**H.R. 1 effects used on the site:** Medicaid cuts (OHA), SNAP cost shift (DHS), no ACA
credit extension (OID), 2017 tax cuts permanent plus tip/overtime/senior deductions through
2028, higher crop reference prices, $5T debt-limit increase, CBO ~$3.4T added deficits.

## 5. What persuasion research says, and how the site applies it

- Broockman & Kalla 2016 (Science) and Kalla & Broockman 2020 (APSR): nonjudgmental,
  two-way conversations that elicit the other person's experience change attitudes
  durably (3+ months). Talking-point delivery does not. → The site leads with the reader's
  own priorities and gives them the number plus source to hand to someone, not a script.
- Feinberg & Willer 2015 (PSPB): moral reframing in the listener's values is more
  persuasive. → Copy uses stewardship, neighbor, honest scales (Proverbs 11:1), who pays.
- Wood & Porter 2019 (Political Behavior): the backfire effect is rare; corrections work.
  Condescension is what fails. → No "blind," no "cult," no mockery anywhere on the site.
- Nickerson & Rogers 2010 (Psychological Science): plan-making prompts raised turnout.
  → "Make your plan" box, saved locally, copyable to send to a friend.
- Kalla & Broockman 2018 (APSR): general-election ads and contact average near-zero
  persuasion; personal conversation is the lever. → The site is built to arm one person
  talking to another, not to be an ad.
- People's Action 2020 deep-canvass experiment (advocacy-run, not independently
  replicated): ~3 points of vote shift among rural white voters, persisting 4 months.

## 6. Design decisions that follow from the research

- Oklahoma first, national second. The hero is a live countdown to early voting.
- Every fact labeled by source tier: government record / independent analysis /
  candidate's own words / news report. Media reporting is used only where it is the only
  public record and is labeled as such.
- Both governor candidates receive identical treatment: same issue rows, same evidence
  standard, "no verified position" when that is the truth.
- The other side's response is printed under every contested item.
- No scores, no recommendations. The reader picks priorities and sees votes and statements.
- Copy buttons on every fact and a "copy my fact sheet" button, because the medium that
  reaches rural Oklahoma is a text message or a Facebook reply, with a source attached.
